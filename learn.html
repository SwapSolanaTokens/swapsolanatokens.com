Solana Token Safety and Basics

Reading a token address

Every token on Solana has a mint address: a long string of letters and numbers, usually 32 to 44 characters. It is the token's real identity. The name and ticker are just labels that anyone can reuse, which is why look-alike tokens exist.

  • Get the mint address from the project's official website or verified social accounts, never from a reply, a direct message or a random group post.
  • Compare the full address, not only the first and last characters. Scammers create addresses that start and end the same way.
  • On this site, the shortened mint is always shown next to the symbol, and the Token Inspector under the chart has a copy button and links to block explorers.

Mint and freeze authority

Two settings on a token tell you how much control its issuer still has.

  • Mint authority can create more supply. If it is active, the issuer can dilute holders at any time.
  • Freeze authority can freeze token accounts so tokens cannot be moved or sold. If it is active, the issuer can lock holders out.

Some legitimate tokens keep one of these on purpose, for example regulated stablecoins. For a small or new token, an active authority is a reason to be careful. This site flags tokens with an active mint or freeze authority above the swap when it can read them.

Tokens created with the Token-2022 standard can add extra features, such as transfer fees. Read the token's details before you trade it.

Liquidity, price impact and slippage

Liquidity is how much value sits in the pools that traders swap against. With thin liquidity, even a small trade moves the price, and selling later can be hard.

  • Price impact is how far your trade moves the price. The swap widget shows it before you sign.
  • Slippage tolerance is the largest price change you accept between the quote and execution.
  • A very high slippage setting makes failed swaps rarer but lets the final price drift far from the quote. Raise it deliberately, not by default.
Rule of thumb: if the quote is far from the market price, or the price impact is large, stop and find out why before you continue.

Token accounts and rent

Solana keeps each token you hold in its own token account. Creating one requires a small refundable deposit of roughly 0.002 SOL. That is why your SOL balance can drop slightly the first time you receive a new token. If you close an empty token account later, the deposit comes back to you.

Always keep a little SOL in your wallet for network fees and these deposits.

What "verified" does and does not mean

A "Verified by Jupiter" badge means the token is on Jupiter's verified list. It helps you avoid obvious fakes, but it is not an audit and not a safety rating. Verified tokens can still fall to zero, and a token can be unverified simply because it is new.

Protocols, TVL and liquid staking

The Solana Protocols directory on the home page ranks protocols by TVL, the value deposited on Solana, using DeFiLlama's public data. TVL shows size and activity. It does not show whether the code is safe.

  • DEXs and aggregators let you swap tokens. Jupiter, which powers this site's swap, is an aggregator.
  • Lending protocols let you deposit assets to earn interest or borrow against collateral, with liquidation risk if prices move.
  • Liquid staking tokens (LSTs) represent staked SOL and keep earning staking rewards while you hold them. They add smart-contract risk on top of SOL itself.

Before you deposit into any protocol, read its documentation, check for published audits, and start with an amount you can afford to lose.

Common scam patterns

  • Fake airdrops and "claim" sites. Anything that asks you to connect a wallet to claim free tokens is a red flag.
  • Direct messages with links. Real projects and support teams do not message you first.
  • Look-alike tokens and sites. Same name and logo, different mint address or domain.
  • Unexpected signing prompts. If a transaction asks for permissions you do not understand, reject it.
  • Pressure and promises. Guaranteed returns, countdown timers and "send X, get 2X" offers are scams.
  • Anyone asking for your seed phrase. No legitimate service ever needs it.

Wallet hygiene

  • Never share your seed phrase or private key, and never type it into a website.
  • Keep long-term savings in a separate wallet from the one you use to try new apps.
  • Read every approval your wallet shows before you sign it.
  • Bookmark the sites you use instead of following links from messages or ads.
  • Keep your wallet app and browser up to date.

FAQ

What is a mint address and why does it matter?

A mint address is the unique on-chain ID of a token on Solana. Names and tickers can be copied by anyone, so two different tokens can both be called the same thing. The mint address cannot be duplicated, so it is the only reliable way to be sure you are trading the token you mean.

Does "Verified by Jupiter" mean a token is safe?

No. It means the token is on Jupiter's verified list, which is a useful filter but not a safety guarantee. Verified tokens can still lose value, and prices can fall sharply. Always check the mint address, liquidity and the token's own official channels.

What are mint authority and freeze authority?

Mint authority is the power to create more supply of a token. Freeze authority is the power to freeze token accounts so they cannot move tokens. Many legitimate tokens have both disabled, and some have a reason to keep them, but an active authority means the issuer can change the rules after you buy, so it deserves a closer look.

What is slippage and price impact?

Price impact is how much your own trade moves the market price, and it grows with trade size and thin liquidity. Slippage tolerance is the largest price change you accept between the quote and execution. If the price moves past it, the swap does not go through.

What is TVL?

TVL, or total value locked, is the value of assets deposited in a protocol. It is a rough measure of size and usage, not of safety. A protocol with a high TVL can still have bugs or be exploited.

Why does my wallet show a small SOL deposit when I receive a new token?

Solana stores each token you hold in its own token account, which needs a small refundable rent deposit, about 0.002 SOL. If you later close an empty token account, that deposit is returned to you.

What should I do if I think I signed something malicious?

Stop interacting with the site, move your remaining assets to a new wallet created from a fresh seed phrase, and revoke token approvals using a reputable tool. Never share your seed phrase with anyone offering help.
